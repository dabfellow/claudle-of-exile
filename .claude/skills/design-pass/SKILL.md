# Design pass

1. **Read first:** `docs/MILESTONES.md` (especially Decisions) and any design doc for this system. Don't ask about anything already decided.
2. **Sort the questions.** Numbers, tuning and small technical calls are yours: decide them on how they play and note each with a one-line reason. Only big, taste-driven choices go to the user.
3. **Ask one topic at a time,** in a numbered message like "Question 4: how maps run", with one to three related questions under it. Number the topics across the whole pass.
4. **Give about two concrete ideas for each question,** labelled A and B, with one or two plain sentences on how each feels to play. Say which you'd pick and why. The user can pick neither.
5. **Weigh options only on how they play.** Don't raise art supply or balance difficulty as costs. Imbalance is partly the point.
6. **Treat the user's examples as examples.** Don't turn them into a fixed list, and don't reopen what they've already said. Maps are essential, so never suggest cutting them.
7. **Use their answers as given.** They often reply in the question's own format, keeping what they want and deleting the rest, so a missing option means no. If they ask "why?" or "who said this?", explain or take the assumption back before moving on. Record notes in their words.
8. **Save as you go.** After each topic, add the answers to `## Decisions` in `docs/MILESTONES.md` or the system's design doc, so an interrupted pass loses nothing.
9. **Finish** with a short summary of what was decided and what you decided on their behalf, then add the build work to `docs/MILESTONES.md` as `- [ ]` items.
