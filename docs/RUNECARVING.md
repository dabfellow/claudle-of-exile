# Rune-carving: three alternatives

Design pass 2026-11-21. Part of the per-skill review in [Skilling Progression](SKILLING_PROGRESSION.md). Rune-carving today: six rune families in four grades, one rune slot per item with slot-specific effects, same-family resonance, and the Inscription chase (Inscription Fragments, the Return Inscription route).

**Status: scrapped (2026-11-21).** Owner turned down my first pick (runes that fill with deeds) and all three alternatives below: "nothing, scrap rune carving." Kept as the record. Rune slots on gear are removed and socketed runes disappear (owner chose, 2026-11-21); the Return Inscription takes its fragments from Cartography boss maps.

All three keep the long-and-short chisel strokes as the active play: hold for the stroke length shown. The right length is a double, close is a hit, too short or too long is weak, and the wrong stroke is a miss.

## Option A — Map runes

Rune-carving becomes the way you rig your endgame farming. You carve runes that are spent on a map before you run it: a Rune of Plenty (more monsters), a Rune of the Hoard (more currency), a Rune of Wrath (harder monsters, better loot), a Rune of Echoes (the boss returns once). A map takes up to three runes. Runes are used up, so carving keeps a steady supply. Gear runes and resonance stay as they are.

- **Idle and offline:** carve rune stock from saved orders.
- **Steady chase:** each rune family ranks up with use, making its effect stronger.
- **Levels 80–99:** greater runes that bend a map's rules, such as every pack having a rare monster.
- **Rare chase:** a master rune that isn't used up for ten maps.
- **Main screen:** the rune stock on shelves, the carving slab, and a "rig a map" panel.
- **Coming back:** new runes on the shelves.
- **What it's for:** endgame juice. Rune-carving decides how rewarding each map is.

## Option B — Bound boss runes

Rune-carving captures the power of bosses you beat. Each boss drops a trace (an echo of its slam, its fire breath, its summoned adds). The carver binds a trace into a rune, and socketed in gear it gives you that boss's move as a proc: "10% chance on hit to cast the Drowned King's Tidal Slam". Every boss in the game is something to collect.

- **Idle and offline:** binding takes time and runs while you're away.
- **Steady chase:** the bestiary of bound runes. Each boss's rune ranks up with more traces.
- **Levels 80–99:** pinnacle boss runes with the strongest moves.
- **Rare chase:** a perfect trace that binds with a second effect from the same boss.
- **Main screen:** a wall of boss silhouettes, lit for the ones you've bound, with the slab beside it.
- **Coming back:** finished runes ready to socket.
- **What it's for:** a reason to beat every boss, with powers that feel like trophies.

## Option C — Rune words

Runes become letters. Each item has a few rune slots, and runes socketed in the right order spell a rune word with a big effect, such as Ember + Ward + Swift spelling "Wildfire". Rune words aren't listed. Some appear as hints in loot and lore, and the rest are found by trying. The six families stay as the letters, and grades make the words stronger.

- **Idle and offline:** carving keeps the letter stock up.
- **Steady chase:** the word book of every rune word found, with perks for filling it.
- **Levels 80–99:** long rune words of four or five letters, with the strongest effects.
- **Rare chase:** a rare glyph rune that counts as any letter.
- **Main screen:** your gear with its slots, the letter stock, and the word book.
- **Coming back:** new letters carved.
- **What it's for:** a discovery chase that builds into your gear.

## My pick

**A — Map runes.** It gives Rune-carving a clear job in the endgame farming loop, and it pairs naturally with Cartography as the map source. B is the most fun on paper but adds a lot of combat work. C is close to Archaeology's language puzzle.

## Owner's choice

None fit; Rune-carving is scrapped (2026-11-21).
