# Veyrath: main story working outline

Last updated 2026-11-19. The owner subsequently authorized seven-act game integration and progression redesign. The seven chapter groups, renamed zones, world/act paintings and saved narrative journal are implemented; see `Veyrath-zone-conversion.md` for the stable-area mapping. This document retains the narrative treatment and unresolved wider-world decisions. Connective details not represented by implemented quests remain proposals.

## Premise

Cast out by Kroten and left to die at sea, the player washes ashore in Veyrath: a kingdom built by generations of people their former society no longer wanted. The descendants of those exiles built roads, cities and a country worth defending. Thirty years after a rebellion expelled Kroten's infiltrators, its victorious leaders still hold emergency powers over a starving, fragmented kingdom.

Across the land, the Creep offers abundance before destroying the systems people need to survive. Crops grow wildly, roots choke irrigation and settlements disappear beneath growth. The capital keeps a small amount of farmland productive through constant guarded labour. Elsewhere, people depend on irregular deliveries, wreck cargo, local agreements and whatever bargains keep their households alive.

The player's first concern is finding a place in that world. Helping the people who take them in gradually exposes the connection between diverted supplies, Kroten's research and the buried founder whose sacrifice has become a nightmare.

## The sea that turns people home

Owner direction, 2026-11-19: Veyrath is surrounded by a harsh ocean and an uncanny offshore limit that prevents ordinary escape. Beyond some distance from the coast, travellers begin to feel compelled to return. The intended horror is psychological: a crew can come back believing its changed intentions were always its own. Memory loss or altered recollection may erase the wish to leave and the experience of turning back. The precise effect, distance and reliability of testimony remain unresolved; these are not yet fixed rules for every voyage.

Ships have been sent out and failed to return. Other attempted departures may end in an unremembered homecoming. Those outcomes should remain distinct, leaving uncertainty about missing crews instead of declaring every ship destroyed. Exceptional vessels can make crossings and return. This fits the existing Kroten shipboard-device secret: their supposed superior seamanship conceals a means of passing the boundary. Exactly what the devices protect, and who understands that protection, remains to be designed.

Visual direction: beautiful inhabited land enclosed by cold, rough water, with sparse, unnatural returning currents or looping ship wakes hinting at an unseen limit. The world atlas suggests the boundary without revealing its mechanism or drawing a literal wall. The force's relationship to the First Exile's possessive idea of home remains a story connection to develop. Defeating the First Exile does not yet establish that the sea opens. This is recorded lore direction and map artwork, not a newly implemented sailing or memory-loss mechanic.

## Campaign structure: seven acts and mapping

Requested 2026-11-17 and integrated as seven acts. On 2026-11-18 the owner chose to redistribute the existing 70 zones into fuller chapters, with area levels 1–10, 10–19, 19–28, 28–38, 38–47, 47–57 and 57–65, followed by the level-66 First Exile. The stable zone mapping and implemented routes are in `Veyrath-zone-conversion.md`. Narrative details beyond the saved journal interactions below remain working proposals.

### Act 1 — The Wreck Coast

- Setting: dangerous beaches, wrecks and the coastal settlement around Winnie's former inn.
- Player purpose: survive, earn shelter and become useful to the community.
- Main movement: Winnie sponsors the player; salvage, helping residents and clearing nearby paths establish relationships and the coast's economy. Rival salvage claims introduce local codes without dominating the plot.
- Story turn: the expected district food delivery fails to arrive, threatening the home the player has begun to earn.

### Act 2 — The Broken Roads

- Setting: the supply routes and outlying settlements between the coast and the next major town.
- Player purpose: trace the missing delivery and reopen routes people depend on.
- Main movement: path clearing and outpost establishment support the investigation. The supply clerk's retained records reveal that an official redirected the shipment. People along the route expose the gap between capital orders and local survival.
- Story turn: the diverted supplies went to a Kroten research expedition. A capital representative's visit to the next large town offers a lead to pursue.

### Act 3 — In the Capital's Wake

- Setting: a larger functioning town and its surrounding farmland and roads.
- Player purpose: find the representative or the next reliable lead after just missing their departure.
- Main movement: local jobs provide equipment, gold and information. Failed farms beside unnatural abundance reveal the Creep's consequences. Evidence broadens the missing-shipment inquiry beyond one official's theft, without exposing the full Kroten arrangement yet.
- Story turn: an onward delivery provides a reason to travel to the next major settlement; the route passes through a city overtaken by the Creep. The delivery's contents remain open.

### Act 4 — The City That Stayed

- Setting: a substantially larger, once-prosperous city whose streets and institutions are overtaken by growth.
- Player purpose: get through the city and complete the onward task while becoming involved with its remaining inhabitants.
- Main movement: survivors and residents refusing to leave sustain several local stories. Their homes, accounts and surviving records show what Veyrath once built and how the capital became involved in the city's fate. The Creep's effect on an established society becomes tangible.
- Story turn: evidence points toward the mountain research facility as the place to find answers. The exact delivery destination and route from it into the mountain investigation remain open.

### Act 5 — Cairnwatch Institute

- Setting: mountain approaches and the research complex.
- Player purpose: establish what is being studied and why district supplies support it.
- Main movement: evidence connects wartime dismantling, the early exiles' containment and Kroten's stolen records. Research documents and experiments reveal the pursuit of useful abundance and the danger of concentrated stockpiles. Hints of a cure agreement and of the shipboard devices lead onward; the capital confirms the political arrangement's full extent in Act 6.
- Story turn: the experiments are not reliably controlled, and records connect their continuation to decisions in the capital.

### Act 6 — The Capital's Promise

- Setting: the capital, its guarded growing grounds, worker households and places of authority and research.
- Player purpose: uncover who authorized the work and what the rulers intend to do as it fails.
- Main movement: compulsory agricultural labour and elite privilege reveal the cost of keeping the city functioning. The player establishes the extent of the Kroten dealings, the promised cure, Kroten's military objective and the suppressed wartime truth. Different participants need not know every secret.
- Story turn: investigating the spreading loss of control reveals a source beneath the city and a route into the old containment.

### Act 7 — The Guardian Beneath

- Setting: buried structures and the remaining containment below the capital.
- Player purpose: reach the source and stop Veyrath from being further overtaken.
- Main movement: the founding stories resolve into the truth of the First Exile's sacrifice and continued transformation. The guardian remains alive through intense infestation; a promise of belonging has become possession.
- Climax: the player defeats the First Exile. Growth across Veyrath halts, but established infestation does not retreat. The campaign ends with time to rebuild, not a restored continent or an automatically resolved political order.

### Endgame — The Unfinished Maps

The player charts other lands and fights entrenched infestation through ongoing expeditions. Local victories have value while total eradication remains a losing cause. The Architect of Ruin and Void Sovereign offer further histories of purposes consumed by the Creep; their exact placement and the Uber First Exile continuity remain open. Overseas access, the extent of halted growth and the identity of mapping lands need later decisions, preserving the existing world-lore limits until then.

## 1. A place at the table

The player survives the landing and enters a coastal community. Winnie vouches for them and offers a bed in his crowded former inn in exchange for dangerous work. They recover wreck cargo, help residents, clear paths and help establish outposts. Each task builds a practical foothold: routes people can use, somewhere to shelter and people who recognize the newcomer.

The residents have codes and loyalties, but they are not collectively innocent. Winnie feeds his refuge partly by storing stolen goods. Helping these people does not require the player to become their moral judge.

A district food shipment fails to arrive. A supply clerk who believes in honouring agreements kept records showing that an official diverted it. Following those records gives the player an initial reason to seek someone connected to the capital. The cache-recovery dispute remains a minor opening detail rather than the spine of the campaign.

## 2. Following the capital's trail

The player learns that a representative or delegation from the capital is expected in the next large town. This is the working interpretation of the owner's travelling-capital reference; the representative's identity and office remain open.

They arrive too late. While finding the next lead, they work for equipment, money and information, clear dangerous routes and become involved in local needs. Signs of the Creep accumulate: apparent plenty beside failed farms, oversized animals, abandoned homes and stories that do not match official explanations.

The search begins to suggest that missing supplies serve a purpose beyond one official's profit. People connected to the capital are supporting work they will not discuss openly.

## 3. The city that stayed

Asked to carry something onward to the next major town, the player must travel through a city overtaken by the Creep. The delivery's contents remain open. This city is substantially larger and more established than any settlement the player has yet seen, demonstrating what Veyrath built before its collapse.

It is still inhabited. Survivors shelter among overgrown streets; some cannot leave and others refuse to abandon homes, businesses, neighbours or family. Their lives support substantial local stories without making the city a detour from the main narrative.

Here the player sees the Creep as more than encroaching wilderness. It has invaded an entire civic world: streets, waterways, households and livelihoods. Local accounts and surviving evidence reveal more of the capital's involvement and point toward a mountain research facility.

## 4. The mountain research facility

The investigation reaches the place where scattered observations become a coherent history. The early exiles encountered the power and built containment around it. Later generations lost the purpose of those structures. Thirty years ago they dismantled them for wartime stone and metal, damaging the containment and scattering its pieces through the kingdom.

Kroten stole surviving records during its infiltration. Its concealed shipboard devices reproduce a small part of the containment and allow safe crossings; its superiority in seamanship is a cover story. It now wants affected resources to support a vast army and conquest of surrounding nations.

Veyrath's rulers accepted Kroten's research under promises of restored farmland and safe seas. They concealed both their dependence on their former enemy and their knowledge of the wartime damage. The researchers' early successes obscured the delayed danger of stockpiling affected materials: sustained concentrations change surrounding ground and water.

The facility provides leads and evidence directing the player to the capital. Precise experiments, documents and encounters remain to be designed.

## 5. The capital's failing promise

The capital contrasts maintained fields and functioning institutions with the labour and coercion required to sustain them. Families must work compulsory shifts to retain food and shelter. The former rebel leaders still present themselves as the people holding the kingdom together.

The player discovers that the experiments are escaping control and learns the extent of the Kroten arrangements. Officials may know different parts of the truth; the storyline should not make every participant omniscient. Exposure alone cannot resolve the immediate threat.

Investigating further reveals a source of the Creep beneath the city. The route downward brings the present political crisis into contact with the kingdom's founding catastrophe.

## 6. The guardian beneath the city

The First Exile is alive, sustained and transformed by intense infestation. They once helped contain the power and sealed themselves beneath the land to protect the home they founded. Their people remember that genuine sacrifice through guardian stories and offerings.

Confinement did not preserve their purpose unchanged. A home from which nobody could be cast out became a belief that everyone arriving belongs to the land forever. The player confronts a founder whose protection has become possession.

The player defeats the First Exile. This halts the Creep's growth across Veyrath, but does not make the established infestation retreat. Roots remain, altered creatures remain and ruined cities do not rebuild themselves. The victory buys a future without restoring the past.

The precise mechanism connecting this defeat to halted growth remains open. Do not assume it automatically frees sea routes, deposes the rulers, ends Kroten's research or eradicates affected stockpiles.

## 7. Mapping: a war without a final frontier

After the campaign, the player charts routes into other lands and repeatedly fights entrenched infestations. The purpose shifts from discovering one source to holding ground, recovering what can be recovered and resisting a problem too extensive for one final victory.

Individual expeditions can matter even though total eradication is a losing cause. The campaign's achievement remains real: Veyrath has stopped being overtaken. Mapping continues indefinitely because existing infestations outlast their source and extend beyond what the player has yet understood.

The identity, accessibility and location of these other lands remain open. This direction does not automatically establish new outbreaks in Orvessa, contact with Jojo Land or infection in Dabtopia. Dabtopia remains untouched; Jojo Land remains overrun and beyond current contact technology. Kroten and Veyrath are in Orvessa, where Veyrath is the only established foothold at the campaign's outset.

## Endgame figures and continuity to resolve later

The Architect of Ruin sought to repair the containment and became willing to destroy communities to restore its original design. The Void Sovereign sought to end the suffering of uncontrolled growth and became committed to erasing everything capable of suffering. Their contrasting answers to the Creep belong in the larger endgame history; exact placements are not assigned here.

An Uber First Exile encounter already exists in the game. Its narrative relationship to the new campaign defeat must be decided before implementation; this outline does not establish resurrection, echoes or repeated canonical deaths.

The story is about finding a place among flawed people, discovering the price of the arrangements keeping them alive, and winning enough breathing room to continue living. Its expansion is coastal survival, political investigation, a lost kingdom's history, a founding promise corrupted, and finally an ongoing struggle against what remains.
