Side systems

Life skills have two kinds of long-term specialisation: the Life Skills tree, which you choose, and work mastery, which grows automatically as you repeat a kind of work. Heists and Expedition add their own longer loops.

Life Skills tree

You earn one point for every 10 total levels across the eleven real life skills, up to {{data:skill-point-cap}} points. Fishing does not count. The tree cannot be fully completed, so choose the branches that support the work you actually do. Branch Notables require level 15 and 30 in that skill; its Keystone requires level 50. Refunds cost an Orb of Regret and must leave the tree connected.

Work mastery

Finishing an eligible timed action adds its unmodified base time to that work band. Live play, offline play, repeats and Greenhouse completions all count; interrupted work does not. Each band reaches level 100 after {{data:mastery-cap-hours}} hours of nominal practice.

  • Every band gains up to {{data:mastery-speed}} percentage points of speed.
  • Gathering bands gain up to {{data:mastery-yield}}% expected extra ordinary primary yield.
  • Production bands gain up to {{data:mastery-conserve}}% expected conservation of listed ordinary inputs.
  • Heist mastery gives speed only. Rare rewards, keys, maps and access requirements are never multiplied or bypassed.

Heists

Thieving and Marks unlock independently from level 1 without campaign progress; every 10 Mark completions guarantees 1 Rogue's Marker alongside random drops. Heist jobs unlock by Thieving level and clean-exit milestones (early chosen exits count). Spend Markers to run a Heist, choosing in advance how many rooms to attempt and, on equipment jobs, an optional gear category target. Later rooms pay more and have a higher alarm chance; only the final room rolls the vault reward. An alarm ends the job and normally keeps {{data:heist-keeps}}% of your take. Extra Thieving levels reduce alarm chance, but only as far as {{data:heist-stealth-floor}}% of the job's normal chance. At Thieving 65 (after a clean Khan's Tribute exit), Occult Treasury awards Treasury Fragments and Oath Fragments for the exclusive Borrowed Tomorrow amulet (also a 0.5% clean vault jackpot) and Stolen Oath Seal projects.

{{data:heists}}

Choose three rogues from twelve available specialists before starting. Their combined primary specialties define your tactical build, and each also provides secondary bonuses. Selecting a fourth replaces the first; crew changes and talent learning wait until the current heist finishes.

Each completed room grants every participating rogue 25 Field Mastery XP. The next level costs current level × 100 XP, with overflow retained, up to level 100. Levels 1–10 grant 2 percentage points of matching-room success per level, capped at 20. Talent points arrive every five levels after 10, starting at 15: ten points fill a rogue's tree at level 60. Four early talents each add 5 percentage points of matching-room success; five Lucky Pockets talents each add 10% of base room gold to Greed; the final talent grants the rogue's signature. Lookout's Omni-Reveal scouts all rooms automatically; the other signatures bypass their primary room type without spending an ability.

Mentor training costs 5,000 gold and teaches any primary specialty the rogue has not already mastered. That rogue leaves the crew and remains unavailable for two finished heists; alarms count, abandoning does not. Train a spare rogue or select a replacement before starting the next job. Training, mastery and talent progress survive saving.

The vault profile shows Guard-Heavy, Trap-Heavy or Loot-Rich layout, tier, room count and threat (base alarm × rooms). Rooms proceed normally on repeat, including offline. Scout a room to reveal its challenge; scouting adds up to three seconds, reduced by Threat Reveal bonuses. A rogue with the matching primary or mentored specialty may spend one ability per heist: Bypass immediately skips the room safely, leaving ordinary room loot behind but retaining vault rewards, or Greed gives 50% more room gold and ordinary drop chance at an extra 15 percentage points of alarm risk. A failed Greed attempt still spends the ability.

Primary economic and utility bonuses contribute 10%, secondary bonuses 5%. Move Speed, Pathfinding, Trap Bypass and Obstacle Evasion shorten rooms; Alarm Suppression, Alarm Gain Reduction, Stealth Movement and Ambush Detection reduce alarms. Loot Quantity, Loot Enhancement and Market Manipulation increase room gold, Loot Quality and Vault Access improve ordinary drop chances, Crit Hit Boost can double room gold, and Market Manipulation also improves Marker lifting. Matching specialties determine room checks, including Guard Distraction and Guard Takedown. Old hired ranks become twice their rank in Field Mastery levels; Fence becomes Grifter and Cutpurse becomes Pickpocket.

Archaeology and Expedition

Archaeology and Expedition are available without campaign progress. Sites require their Archaeology levels and never run out. Long-term finds occur after {{data:expedition-hours}} base-speed hours at a site and move it through {{data:expedition-strata}}. Each find permanently raises that site's artifact yield by 25% and gives one standing with its culture.

Start at Giant's Cairn. Its first discovery opens the Cutting and Colossus at their listed levels; the Cutting opens Barrow and Adit, and Barrow opens the Heart. Reached sites and finds stay open across saves. Every 60 completed digs of one culture gives one protected relic fragment, including at bedrock. Bonus copies do not advance this counter. Keep fragments for restoration or exchange five for 25 matching artifacts; exchanges never grant standing.

After the first Cairn discovery, Archaeology 15 and the default Forge can restore a Surveyor's Guard: five Survey fragments, 50 Giant Tallies and five Copper Bars, ten minutes of work. Choose Fire, Cold or Lightning resistance; the rare shield guarantees Life and that resistance. It still needs character level 8 and its normal attributes to equip. Sifting supplies one guaranteed artifact every ten seconds without dig, find or standing progress. Colossus, Adit, and Heart discoveries also unlock The Buried Grudge (Archaeology 65), Restored Taboo Tablet, and Convergence Relic projects.

Spend each culture's Artifacts at its merchant. Better wares need standing and shelves refill over time. Standing orders buy again after restocks while you can afford them; they do not create stock or bypass standing.
