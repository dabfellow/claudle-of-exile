Getting started

Path of Idle is an idle action RPG. Your character fights on their own, keeps going while you are away, and you decide what they wear, which skills they use and when those skills fire. If you only want a list of what to do, use the Beginner's checklist.

Pick a class

Each class has one combat mechanic of its own, and later a choice of ascendancy. Warrior is the most forgiving start for idle play. See Classes and ascendancies and Builds and playstyles.

Your first minutes

Old Brannoc, the guide, walks you through these steps and pays you for most of them.

  1. Open the Campaign tab and enter your first area, {{area:mudflats}}.
  2. Your Default Attack swings by itself. Skills wait for you: click one or press Q to Y, or give it a rule on the Skills page so it fires on its own.
  3. Drink your Life Flask with key 1 before you are in trouble, not after. Your Mana Flask is key 2. Flasks refill as you kill.
  4. Equip better gear as it drops. Your first few kills always give you some gear and orbs to start with.
  5. Mine some copper and smith a weapon, then use an Orb of Transmutation to make it magic.
  6. Open your Hideout and build the Crafting Bench, so you can salvage gear you don't want.
  7. Spend your passive point, then beat {{monster:gorger}}.

Areas and bosses

Each area has a boss that appears after a set number of kills. Beating it opens the road onward, and the last boss of an act finishes the act. Bosses have telegraphed moves, so read the area's page first. The Campaign walkthrough lists every area in order, and Boss preparation covers what to bring.

Automation

Skills run on rules, such as "use this when life is low". Simple rules are free. Reactive rules are unlocked at the War Table in your Hideout. Start with Automation basics.

Offline play

Your character keeps going while you are away, up to a limit that starts at 8 hours and can be raised. Offline fighting stops if your character dies. See Offline progression.

Dying

A death costs 5% of the XP towards your next level, but never a level. You retreat from the fight and come back with a quarter of your life. Items on the ground are marked, and a second death loses any you still haven't picked up.
