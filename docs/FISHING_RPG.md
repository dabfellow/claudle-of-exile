# Saltmere Fishing RPG Design

**Design draft · 20 Nov 2026**

Fishing is a complete RPG hidden inside Path of Idle. You arrive in Saltmere with a cheap rod. You can spend your first evening learning a village pond, spend a week outfitting a ship, or spend months hunting the last few entries in a 500-plus-species logbook. The port grows around what you discover. Eventually the same player who once fished from a muddy bank is provisioning a crew for several days beyond the islands.

The owner’s direction establishes shore and ocean fishing, 500-plus distinct catchable fish, genuinely rare species, equipable tackle, customizable boats and crews, AFK traps, and multi-day voyages beyond the boundary that turns early boats back. The logbook, fleet, and voyages are roughly half the game. The other half is the harbor, people, competitions, and mysteries that give players new reasons to use all of it. The counts beyond 500, example species, and timing below are design proposals.

## Entry and first evening

The existing Fishing entry reads “Coming soon” until a character earns the hidden achievement. The earning realm opens Saltmere and shares its progress and prizes. Solo Self-Found remains isolated, and Classic follows its life-skill restriction. The achievement is intended for a nearly maxed character. Its exact requirements are still open. Fishing has its own progression, equipment, and economy. The ordinary skilling tree and total levels stay outside its progression.

Entering Saltmere opens a dedicated game screen with a chart, tackle, fleet, and logbook. Give the waterfront a proper playable view with places to visit, characters to meet, and a shoreline to explore. On the first evening the player borrows a rod, chooses between two walkable waters, lands a fish, and takes it to the harbor. Its logbook page fills in, a fishmonger offers to buy it, and a local angler points out a catch that takes a different bait. Soon the player can set a trap before leaving. The opening should feel like arriving in a new game with a world worth exploring.

## Waters and the world chart

Freshwater, brackish water, and saltwater each have their own families, tools, and reasons to revisit. The chart shows individual waters, landing points, depths, local observations, and routes that the current boat can reach. Unknown waters can appear as rumors or partial chart entries. Named waters need recognizable shorelines, distinct encounter pools, and at least one discovery or challenge of their own.

| Stage | Waters | What expands |
|---|---|---|
| On foot | Village ponds, mill streams, rivers, marshes, inland lakes | Bank positions, floats, lures, freshwater traps, and the first harbor relationships |
| First boat | An estuary, lake islands, river mouths, tidal flats, and sheltered bays | Anchoring, short routes, pots, baitfish, and the first crew berth |
| Inside the boundary | Reefs, kelp beds, sea caves, island channels, and wrecks | Wind, tide, depth, current, specialized gear, and choosing how much haul to risk |
| Beyond the boundary | Open ocean, seamounts, pelagic waters, and far shoals | Multi-day routes, provisions, crew watches, longlines, and fish that move between grounds |
| Pinnacle waters | The abyss, seasonal storm grounds, and waters found through mysteries | Multi-trip pursuits, rare conditions, legendary fights, and the strangest prizes |

A bigger boat opens new grounds. Bank fishing remains the best way to reach many freshwater fish. Late freshwater hunts can require patience, light tackle, and local clues that the strongest ocean rig does little to help with.

## The logbook and the 500-plus fish

Aim for **540 distinct species** across roughly 35 authored waters. Count a species once wherever it appears. Size, coloration, and trophy variants live inside its entry. Use a mixture of real fish, Veyrath-native fish, and bizarre late-game discoveries. A player should be able to remember where they caught something and what made it different.

| Species group | Working count |
|---|---:|
| Ponds, streams, and rivers | 95 |
| Lakes, reservoirs, and upland waters | 90 |
| Marshes, estuaries, and tidal flats | 75 |
| Coasts, islands, reefs, and wrecks | 110 |
| Open ocean and deep sea | 135 |
| Secret and legendary waters | 35 |
| **Total** | **540** |

A species entry records habitat, conditions, baits that work, encounter method, fight behavior, first catch, count, best weight, and records such as a clean release. Entries progress from unknown to rumored to confirmed. Early pages give players leads without filling every empty slot with an exact answer. The journal can filter by water, family, catch method, availability, and incomplete collections. Pin a fish and see its confirmed leads beside the chart and equipment screen.

Most fish build the texture and economy of a water. Uncommon fish give reasons to change bait or depth. Rare fish have low chances **even after the player finds the right conditions**. As a tuning starting point, a local rare might take roughly one in 100 eligible bites and a special might take one in 1,000 or fewer. Give those fish distinctive appearances, behavior, and rewards. Legends have their own encounters and can combine rarity, investigation, and execution. Where a port or chapter requires progress, credit an obtainable body of evidence so the story can advance while the rarest entries remain a long-term chase.

For example, a **Glassfin Tench** might bite in a silted millpond after rain, near reeds, on an insect bait. Its fight is mostly a light touch followed by sudden sideways bursts. An open-ocean **Lanternback** might follow a cold front across two grounds, show up on a night watch, and put a heavy rod through three long dives. Both are standalone entries with particular reasons to go looking for them.

## Active fishing and passive traps

Active fishing begins by reading a spot and choosing where and how to cast. A float works the surface, a lure retrieves through cover, and a weighted rig reaches fish beneath the boat. Bait and presentation shape what bites. Once hooked, read the fish’s tells, manage drag and tension, brace against runs, and decide when to reel. Small fish can come in quickly. Opponents with different habits should ask for different responses. A deep diver leans on line capacity while a reed fish tries to reach cover. Legendary fights change behavior during the encounter. Phone and desktop controls need the same readability and room for skilled play.

**AFK traps catch real fish.** Set and bait traps in eligible waters and collect their haul later. Different designs catch different species, including a few trap specialists with their own rare entries. Creels, pots, and nets have finite space, bait, and soak times. The deployment screen shows likely catch families, when the gear will be ready, how much it can hold, and any local risk. Accounts can work multiple traps as they earn permits and equipment. Traps support fishmongers, bait supply, and logbook completion. Hook-and-line trophies and legendary fights still require an active landing. A player can enjoy a night of setting pots or spend that time out fighting a fish themselves.

Recorded catches can be released, sold, kept for recipes, or donated where appropriate. Catch records remain after the fish leaves the inventory. A clean release can carry its own record or tournament value.

## Equipment, boatbuilding, and crew

**Angler equipment** is owned, equipped, and stored. Rods define flex, casting method, and fighting leverage. Lines trade strength and capacity against visibility and sensitivity. Reels change drag, retrieval, and recovery under strain. Hooks, floats, and lures alter presentation. Bait is a consumable with actual supply routes through harvesting, shops, traps, and recipes. Progression unlocks equipment through crafting, discoveries, fishing relationships, and competitions. Presets make it easy to switch from a light lake outfit to trolling without rebuilding every slot.

**Boats are builds in their own right.** A boat has a hull class and a fit-out. Wood affects weight, resilience, and handling. Sails change speed and which winds are favorable. Oars control shallow, narrow, or windless approaches. Fit the rest of the boat for the job, with choices such as an anchor, livewell, cargo space, or a protected locker. A light skiff might reach a reed channel an offshore vessel cannot. A reinforced cutter buys more room for mistakes among reefs. A long-range ship can carry stores for several days but needs more provisioning. The fleet screen shows what changed and whether the selected boat can run the plotted voyage.

**Crew have named jobs and specialties.** Hire a navigator for route options, a deckhand for handling and repairs, a cook for provisions, or a spotter for finding schools and signs. Berths force choices. A navigator who knows a dangerous crossing matters more early than later, when a survey already revealed a safe route. Crew stories and completed voyages earn trust and new abilities. The player can keep several boats outfitted for different jobs.

## The boundary and multi-day voyages

Early fishing trips have room to explore within a few miles of the islands. The first attempt to push farther should make the boundary tangible. A heading bends back toward familiar water, or a returning current defeats the crossing even in good sailing weather. The player records that experience and finds a lead. Unlocking offshore routes requires a discovery and a voyage that demonstrates safe passage. Hull improvements help with the journey but cannot solve the boundary by themselves.

Saltmere already fits Veyrath’s northwest coast. Kroten’s concealed shipboard devices provide an established thread to investigate. The effect on fishing craft occurs on the local boundary and leaves the fate of Veyrath’s missing escape ships unresolved. Beyond-boundary fishing opens sea grounds. Any introduction of foreign ports or travel to other nations is a separate world decision. [Veyrath map](VEYRATH-MAP.md) · [Story](STORY.md) · [Lore decisions](MILESTONES.md)

Treat an offshore voyage as a journey with legs. Before departure, plot a route, fit the ship, pick a crew, and load bait, food, and repair supplies. See the forecast, range, last safe return point, and expected duration. On each leg the ship sails, the crew keeps watch, and the chart accumulates consequences. On arrival at a ground, choose what to fish for or where to go next. A broken spar, a shadow under the surface, or a report of better water should change the decision you planned at the dock.

**Proposed timing** is real elapsed time for longer passages, with a first offshore voyage spanning roughly two to three days and giving the player several playable stops. The ship can sail and crew can work while the player is away. Important decisions and active fights wait at safe checkpoints, so sleeping through the night never makes the game choose a bad route or lose a rare bite for you. Traps and harbor work stay available as reasons to check in between legs. Short shore sessions and local boat trips give players something they can start and finish in one sitting.

On short voyages the protected locker can save a few catches from a wreck. Deep expeditions put the unlanded haul at stake and show the exposure before departure. Safe harbors are opportunities to land a haul, resupply, or turn home. A failed voyage can cost supplies and unbanked fish. Permanent boats, equipped tackle, and earned logbook records survive. Once a voyage is banked, a reload or reconnect credits it only once.

### Example voyage

You leave Saltmere in a shallow-draft cutter with a navigator and a deckhand. The first afternoon you stop inside the islands and fill a livewell with baitfish. During the first night the crew follows a charted passage across the boundary. The next day you can spend the fresh bait at a seamount where something large is feeding, or take a safer stop and bank the catches you already have.

You choose the seamount. The deckhand keeps a damaged fitting together through the next leg. A clue from the ship’s night watch narrows the depth for a fish you’ve been tracking. You land it with the heavy outfit, then decide whether the last of your provisions can get you to one more ground. The return to Saltmere completes its logbook entry, gives the fishmonger a new recipe, and earns a referral to an angler investigating the same species.

## The other half of the RPG

**The harbor grows around discoveries.** The tackle shop, smokehouse, fish market, nursery, yard, and research room gain people, services, and visible changes from the fish, materials, and stories the player brings back. A boatwright might need timber from a newly reached inlet and evidence that a hull will survive a particular current. The resulting boat part has a clear use on the chart. Let players fund different projects first and see changes in the waterfront.

**People, contracts, and competitions give old waters new rules.** A family teaches a method in return for help finding a lost fishery. A restaurant wants fresh catches from several waters before its next feast. A pond competition limits everyone to basic rods and scores the variety of clean releases. A crew championship asks two people with different specialties to work the same offshore route. Keep ordinary fishing available alongside rotating events, with access to equivalent prizes when an event returns.

**Mysteries join different systems.** A remarkable fish can be the start of a quest. An odd catch from a pond might resemble a juvenile from a sailor’s sketch. Bringing together the two logbook pages, talking to a specialist, and taking the right crew on a survey reveals a new ground. Long mysteries can cross freshwater, traps, boatbuilding, and multiple expeditions. Stage discoveries so they remain interesting after the player has the strongest general-purpose gear.

**Long-term play has more than one finish line.** Complete a favorite water, keep family records, finish a harbor storyline, win a competition, outfit the entire fleet, hunt down the last rare char, or complete the full logbook. Give exceptional catches and feats a visible home in the harbor. Let record hunters pursue weight, unusual variants, clean catches, and difficult tackle restrictions after they land a species for the first time.

## Rewards and progression

Fish pay for better tackle, boats, bait, crew, harbor projects, and new leads. Major rewards change how fishing works. A reel could catch a broken line once per fight, a livewell could keep a rare bait alive across several legs, and a late sail might let a weather window reach one extra ground.

Some trophies unlock deliberately overpowered rewards for ordinary Path of Idle. **Crownfire Feast** and **Bottomless Tide**, the self-refilling combat flask, are examples from the existing prototype. The fishing realm earns the recipes and licenses, can share the resulting prizes within that realm, and keeps them out of the Player Market. Players are likely at the far end of the main game by then. Give them toys worth using even when they have little left that needs beating.

## Build order and acceptance

1. **Make the first evening feel like a separate game.** Build the chart, an illustrated shore and harbor, a real equip screen, a useful logbook, bank fishing, and a working trap loop. Test whether a new player can discover their next goal by playing.
2. **Build the nearshore campaign.** Add harbor projects, role-specific crew, fit different boats for different jobs, and make tides, approach, and location change the fish available. Include one tournament and one mystery that send players back inland.
3. **Build the boundary and offshore campaign.** Deliver the first successful crossing and a multi-day return voyage with safe checkpoints, meaningful provisioning, rescue and wreck outcomes, and jobs that settle once when the player returns.
4. **Author the full catalog and long pursuits.** Verify 500-plus unique, reachable species, plausible habitat pools, trap and active catches, genuine low-rate specials, and entries players can reason about from in-game evidence. Finish the harbor, competitions, mysteries, and fishing and combat rewards before treating the RPG as complete.

Use a development-only route to play the entire RPG while the secret achievement is undecided. The existing five-water fishing experience is the prototype for controls, rewards, and realm saves. Completion means experiencing the full arc from first shore catch to multi-day voyages, with the harbor, mysteries, and logbook still giving players reasons to come back after the final crossing.

## Decisions to settle before building the dependent systems

- The secret achievement’s exact condition and when to activate it.
- Whether a first crossing uses a recovered device, an improvised equivalent, or another solution consistent with the established shipboard technology.
- How long the real-time waits should be in the first offshore route and how often players get substantial playable decisions during longer voyages. The two-to-three-day first voyage is a playtest starting point.
- Which kinds of truly rare catches may come out of traps, and which trophies need a rod. Confirm examples early enough to shape the catalog and the logbook.
